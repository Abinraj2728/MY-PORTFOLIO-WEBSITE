James Webb Space Telescope: Surprising Discoveries

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has revealed unexpected findings since it began science operations. One major surprise is a population of compact, bright red galaxies called "Little Red Dots." These objects appeared around 600 million years after the Big Bang and then rapidly declined in number less than a billion years later. Astronomers are still investigating what powers them—possibly dense star clusters or growing black holes. Webb also found galaxies that formed far earlier and were brighter than models predicted, challenging assumptions about how quickly the early universe assembled structure.

Why This Matters

Beyond early galaxies, Webb has studied exoplanet atmospheres with unprecedented clarity, detecting molecules like carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide. It has also provided independent data on the "Hubble Tension"—the disagreement between different measurements of the universe's expansion rate—showing that the discrepancy is unlikely to be a simple measurement error. These observations are reshaping our understanding of cosmic dawn, planetary habitability, and the fundamental parameters of the universe.
